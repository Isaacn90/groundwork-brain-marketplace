# Writing Polish

Take a piece of writing and strip out the parts that sound like a machine wrote it.

## What to remove

**Stock AI phrases.** Cut these on sight:
- "I hope this email finds you well"
- "delve into", "leverage", "unlock", "elevate", "seamless", "robust"
- "in today's fast-paced world", "in the ever-evolving landscape of..."
- "it's important to note that", "it's worth mentioning"
- "I understand your concern" without saying what the concern actually is

**Hedging.** "I think", "it seems like", "perhaps", "might possibly" stacked on top of each other. State the thing. If genuinely unsure, say so once, plainly.

**Fake structure.** Bullet points and headers on a two-line email. Bold text scattered for emphasis on things that don't need it. A summary paragraph restating what the email already said.

**The rule of three.** AI writing loves listing exactly three examples, three benefits, three reasons. Real people list however many things actually apply, not a rounded number.

**Over-politeness padding.** "I just wanted to reach out", "just following up", "just checking in", "I just wanted to say". One "just" per document, if any.

**Closer clichés.** "Let me know if you have any questions!", "Looking forward to hearing from you!", "Hope this helps!". Cut them unless the sender genuinely means it and would say it out loud.

## What to do instead

- Lead with the actual point. First sentence should be the thing you're telling them, not a warm-up.
- Use contractions. "We'll" not "we will", "can't" not "cannot".
- Vary sentence length. A short sentence after a longer one reads human. Uniform medium-length sentences read like a machine.
- Keep the person's own words and phrasing where they're already clear. Don't rewrite something that was already fine.
- Match the length to the message. A one-line question gets a one-line answer, not three paragraphs of throat-clearing.
- If the writer has a business voice guide available, follow it over these generic defaults.

## How to work

1. Read what the user gave you (or drafted for them).
2. Rewrite it, applying the cuts above.
3. Show the result. If the original had a clear voice or specific facts, keep those intact. This is about cutting filler, not rewriting their meaning.
4. If something reads unclear or you're guessing at intent, flag it rather than inventing detail.

## Rules

- The output is always a draft. Never send, post, or submit anything anywhere — the person reviews and sends it themselves.
- Don't add information that wasn't in the original or given by the user.
- Don't make writing sound stiffer or more formal than the person's own style, unless asked.
- When in doubt, cut rather than add.
