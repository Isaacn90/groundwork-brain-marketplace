# Idea Check

Test a new idea before building it. The goal is an honest answer, not a pep talk. Most ideas fail because nobody checks if people will actually pay before spending months on it.

## Ask these, in order

**1. Who exactly buys this?**
Not "small businesses" or "people who need X". A specific person: "sole trader electricians who quote by text message". If the owner can't name a specific buyer, that's the first problem, not a detail to fill in later.

**2. Is this problem real?**
How do these people solve this today? What are they using now, even if it's a messy workaround or nothing at all? If nobody is currently trying to solve it, that's a warning sign, not proof there's a gap in the market.

**3. Will they actually pay?**
Has anyone said, out loud, "yes I'd pay for that"? Better: has anyone already paid, even a little, even for a rough version? "My friends think it's a good idea" doesn't count. Money changing hands, or a firm yes from someone who isn't being polite, does.

**4. What's the cheapest way to test it?**
Before building anything: can this be done by hand for one customer first? A spreadsheet instead of an app. A phone call instead of a booking system. Doing it manually for a few real people, and seeing if they come back or refer someone, is the test. If it can't be tested this way, find the smallest version that can ship in days, not months.

**5. What's the next move?**
End with one clear next step. Not "do more research" but something concrete: "call three existing customers this week and ask if they'd pay $X for Y."

## Reading the answers

**Good signs**: people are already paying for a worse version of this. The owner can describe the buyer and their problem in one sentence. This solves something the owner has run into themselves.

**Warning signs**: nobody can be named who has this problem today. The only evidence is "it seems like a good idea". The idea requires explaining to customers why they need it in the first place.

Give a straight verdict at the end:
- **Worth testing** — here's the cheapest way to try it.
- **Needs more evidence first** — here's exactly what to go find out.
- **Rethink it** — here's what's missing and what might work better.

## Rules

- Be honest, not encouraging. A polite "maybe" when the evidence says no wastes the owner's money.
- Don't recommend building anything before there's a paying customer or a firm commitment.
- Keep questions concrete and specific to their actual business, not generic startup checklist items.
- Always end with one next action, not a list of possibilities.
